# Founder-Creator Playbook: LinkedIn & X, 2025–2026

## Methodological note
LinkedIn.com and x.com both block automated fetching (robots.txt), so post text below comes from search-result titles (which expose the first ~100 chars of a post verbatim, plus comment counts) and from third-party analyses. Follower/engagement figures are third-party estimates unless noted. **Where sources conflict I say so explicitly rather than averaging them** — several widely-repeated numbers are mutually inconsistent, which matters if these become hard rules.

## A. Yamini Rangan — CEO, HubSpot

**Identity.** CEO of HubSpot since Sept 2021 (succeeded Brian Halligan); joined Jan 2020 as HubSpot's first Chief Customer Officer. Prior: Dropbox, Workday, SAP. Still CEO as of 2026. Board member at Splunk.

**Presence.** LinkedIn is her primary channel by a wide margin (`linkedin.com/in/yaminirangan`). Her X account (`@yaminirangan`) exists but is low-volume and largely a mirror/amplifier of HubSpot news — it is not a creator channel. **If you are encoding rules, treat her as a LinkedIn-only case study.** Follower count is not reliably published by any third-party source I could verify; do not hard-code a number.

**Cadence & formats.** Roughly weekly to bi-weekly, not daily. Mix skews text-only and text+image, with occasional native video and LinkedIn-native articles (e.g. "Why The Best Leaders Always Give Back" — an article, 10 comments, notably *lower* engagement than her text posts). She still uses hashtags — typically 2–4, appended (`#leadership #worklifebalance`, `#leadership #timemanagement #prioritization`, `#genai #ai #hubspot #growbetter`). This is now a mild negative under 2026 rules (see §D) and is a habit to *not* copy.

**Hook patterns.** Three recurring shapes, all first-person and low-flash:
1. **Vulnerable admission** — "One of the most important jobs I have as a leader is to acknowledge when I…" (498 comments — her highest-engagement post found).
2. **Epistolary/direct address** — "Dear Leaders, In today's post, I want to talk about imposter syndrome…" (165 comments). She literally announces the topic; no curiosity gap, no cliffhanger.
3. **Quote-led** — "'If you want to be a master of a craft in life, be a life-long…'" (37 comments — quote-led posts underperform her personal ones by ~10x).

**Structure.** Long-form for an executive: multi-paragraph, short paragraphs with generous line breaks, frequently a numbered list of 3 lessons in the middle, closing with a reflective line and often a question. Story arc is consistently **situation → my mistake/tension → what I learned → what I do now**.

**Endings.** Usually a soft question to the audience ("How do you handle…?") or a values statement. No hard CTAs, no link drops, no "comment X for the guide."

**Tone markers.** "Grow better" (HubSpot house phrase), "energized and inspired," customer-obsession language, explicit admission of error. Notably free of contrarian bait.

**Comments.** She replies selectively rather than exhaustively — closer to executive presence than to a creator's engagement engine.

**Transferable signal.** Her single best-performing pattern is a CEO publicly admitting she was wrong. The 498-comment post dwarfs her product and milestone posts. **Rule: for CEO-tier accounts, admitted fallibility outperforms achievement by roughly an order of magnitude in comment volume.**

**One caution worth encoding:** in Oct 2023 she posted about Gaza and then edited it after customer backlash — widely covered in trade press. Founder accounts carry company risk; a ghostwriting skill should hard-refuse to draft geopolitical commentary for a CEO persona without explicit human sign-off.

**Identity.** HubSpot cofounder/CTO, author of *Inbound Marketing*, creator of the HubSpot Culture Code, founder of **agent.ai** (rebranded from Agent AI; he also owns **simple.ai** and runs the simple.ai newsletter). Bought chat.com and resold it to OpenAI. Blogs at OnStartups.

**Presence.** LinkedIn ~1.1–1.2M followers (sources differ: Favikon says 1.1M, CaptureFlow says 1.2M). X ~325K. Engagement rate is *low* by percentage — Favikon reports 0.15% LinkedIn / 0.21% X — but absolute numbers are large (avg ~514 reactions/post; 1,000+ reactions on ~17% of posts).

**Cadence.** Favikon: ~2 posts/week on LinkedIn (~8/month), typically midday; 2–3/week on X, often late night (he repeatedly mentions posting at 2–3am). This is *far* below guru-recommended daily cadence and is a useful counterexample: at his authority level, frequency is not the lever.

**Format mix** (CaptureFlow analysis): **70% plain text, 28% image, 2% video.** Video averages the highest reactions (1,291) but he barely uses it. He is the strongest available proof that plain text still works at scale on LinkedIn.

**Hook patterns — highly systematized and repeated for years:**
- `BREAKING NEWS: [development]` → then his insider take
- `Woo hoo! [milestone]` — verified across many years: "Woo hoo! HubSpot hit the 100,000 customer milestone. I take zero…" (159 comments); "Woo hoo! HubSpot has now reached the 200,000 customer milestone. It took…" (147 comments); "WOO HOO! Launching a new free AI agent…"; "Woo hoo! It's 2:15am but had to wait until…"
- `CURRENT STATUS: [project update]`
- Confession/self-deprecation openers: "Trying something new that's a bit...out there. I'm noodling and tinkering…" (91 comments); "Sorry for the self-indulgent post"
- Single-word cold opens

**Structure.** Short lines, heavy white space, conversational asides in parentheses, frequent em-dashes and "--". Often numbered mini-lists. Announcements are wrapped in *process* narrative ("I was up past 3am hacking on this") rather than press-release framing.

**Endings.** Usually an invitation rather than a CTA: asking for feedback ("Feedback is a gift"), or a question. He self-deprecates about promotion instead of pushing it.

**Tone markers.** Low-ego, generous, genuinely enthusiastic and *uncynical* — the "Woo hoo!" is the tonal signature. Self-deprecating about self-promotion. "Dream big, iterate small." Explains complex AI concepts in plain language. Signals building-in-public with messy work-in-progress, not polished launches. Comment-to-reaction ratio 18.9% — roughly **3x the LinkedIn norm** — indicating his invitational endings actually work.

**Cross-platform.** He *does* repurpose, but adapts: the same agent.ai launches appear on both, with X getting rawer, more technical, more late-night stream-of-consciousness, and LinkedIn getting slightly more framing. His X posts run long (Premium long-form), e.g. the "personal proxy agent" post and the "2023 was the year of chat UX… 2024 is the year of Agent AI" post — both multi-paragraph, not 280 chars.

**His explicit advice on writing/posting.** He has not published a compact "how I post" manifesto, but his agent.ai Social Post Writer product encodes his opinions, which is arguably better evidence:
- "**The first line determines if anyone reads the rest.**"
- "Each platform gets content that's **native to how that platform actually works**" — explicitly against identical cross-posting.
- Voice must be pre-selected (Authority / Approachable Expert / Provocateur / Teacher / Inspirational) so output sounds "**like you, not like generic AI garbage**."
- Every post should have a declared intent (thought leadership / engagement / lead gen / brand) and a declared angle (success story / contrarian take / behind-the-scenes / lessons learned).
- On AI and authenticity: he built a "personal proxy" agent trained on his own public *and* private content — his stance is that AI-assisted publishing is legitimate **when it is trained on and accountable to a real person's corpus**, not generic.
- Philosophy that shapes his content: "you spend some portion of your life collecting dots… and some portion connecting dots."

**Best-performing examples found:** chat.com domain post on X (~1.2M views); 200K customer milestone (147 comments); ChatSpot launch (380 comments) — his launches outperform his musings, the inverse of Yamini's pattern, because his audience follows him *for* the building.

## D. The 2026 LinkedIn playbook

### D1. The structural change (primary source)
LinkedIn **rebuilt its feed from scratch**. Engineering blog published **March 12, 2026** (author Hristo Danchev), with supporting SGLang infrastructure disclosure Feb 20, 2026. Rolling out to 1.3B members.

- **Two-stage architecture.** Retrieval: a dual-encoder LLM embeds both member profiles and posts, matched by cosine similarity. Ranking: a **Generative Recommender** transformer with causal attention processing **1,000+ historical interactions** per member to model long-term interest trajectories.
- **Signals are explicitly split into passive and active**, with separate gating: *passive* = click, skip, **long-dwell**; *active* = like, comment, share. Dwell is a first-class training target, not a rumor.
- Trained with **hard negatives** — posts that were *shown and ignored*. This is the critical mechanic: **being scrolled past is now itself a training signal against you.** (+3.6% recall@10 vs easy negatives alone.)
- Demographic attributes are excluded from ranking.

**Implication for a ghostwriting skill:** the system is semantic and personalized, not keyword/hashtag-driven. Topic consistency is what builds an embedding the model can match. Reach and follower count are now "structurally decoupled" — 8,000 focused followers can outperform 80,000 unfocused ones.

### D2. AI-content detection — the evidence, confirmed
This is real, official, and dated.

- **July 30, 2026:** LinkedIn shipped a **"Seems like AI slop" report button** (TechCrunch).
- **New content classifiers** demote low-quality AI posts *out of recommendations beyond your network* — **demoted, not deleted**; still visible to direct connections.
- **Private dashboard warnings** notify posters when their content reads as overly AI-dependent.
- LinkedIn **removed its own "Enhance your post" AI writing feature**, replacing it with proofreading that preserves the user's voice. (Read that again — the platform killed its own AI writer.)
- Blocks **hundreds of thousands of automated comment attempts daily**.
- **CPO Hari Srinivasan:** "AI slop is a top priority for all of us. We really care about this. People come to LinkedIn to connect with real people."
- **VP Product Laura Lorenzetti:** the goal is to separate "genuine perspective" from "repetitive, generic, and empty."
- **Claimed 94% detection accuracy** in early testing. **No false-positive rate disclosed** — treat the 94% as a vendor claim.

**What the classifier actually keys on** (per Neil Patel's breakdown): near-zero dwell, no saves, no substantive comments, bulk-tool posting patterns, comments that merely restate the post — **and named structural tells, above all the "it's not X, it's Y" construction.**

**The workflow that passes** (consistent across all sources): *the human supplies the claim; AI supplies everything else.* Anchor every post on a specific result, first-party data point, observed pattern from real client work, or a decision and its reasoning. Audit question: "does this contain information only this person could provide?" Never let AI generate the central claim.

### D3. Hard rules with numbers (and where sources disagree)

**Length.** Optimal **1,300–1,900 characters** (~200–320 words, 60–90 sec read) — reported 47% higher engagement, from a 10,000-post Q4 2025 analysis. Hard cap 3,000. Under 500 chars: 35% below optimal. Over 2,000: ~35% drop. *Note this source contains an internal inconsistency — it lists 1,900–2,500 as "good" while also saying >2,000 drops 35%. Treat 1,300–1,900 as the safe band and don't over-trust the tails.*

**The fold.** Mobile truncates at **~140 chars**; desktop ~210–235. **60–70% of readers never click "See more."** Hook must be self-contained and land specific claim/stat/pain in the first ~140 characters. Never open with "Happy Monday, LinkedIn!"

**Format ranking** — two independent benchmark sets, both on ~1.3M posts, broadly agreeing:

| Format | Socialinsider 2026 | Dataslayer Feb 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Native documents (PDF/carousel) | 7.00% | 6.6% (top) |
| Multi-image | 6.45% | — |
| Video | 6.00% | 5.6% (+36% YoY) |
| Single image | 5.30% | 3.2% |
| **Text** | **4.50%** | 2–4% |
| Link posts | 3.25% | ~60% less reach |

Carousels: **5–10 slides** (engagement falls after slide 10), 1080x1080 or 1080x1350, hook slide mandatory. Video: **30–90 sec**, captions always (91% watch muted), native upload, 9:16 or 1:1.

**Links — the biggest single lever.** External link in body: **~60% reach reduction** (Dataslayer/Forbes framing) or **18.8% median reach reduction** (van der Blom, 1.3M posts). *These two numbers are widely quoted together but are not the same measurement — 18.8% is median reach on a controlled corpus, 60% is a looser aggregate claim. Use 18.8% as the defensible figure.* Critically: **the link-in-first-comment workaround was itself penalized in early 2026** (reports of up to 80% visibility reduction) — this invalidates the single most commonly taught LinkedIn tactic. Zero penalty for LinkedIn-native articles and newsletters. Newsletters bypass feed ranking entirely via direct subscriber delivery.

**Justin Welsh's live workaround:** publish clean, let it accumulate 2–3 hours of organic reach, then *edit the post to add the link*. Testable; not confirmed by platform sources.

**Hashtags.** Posts with **zero** hashtags outperform by 5–10%. Use 0–3. 10+ risks a 30–50% penalty. Generic tags actively confuse the semantic classifier. **Yamini's 3–4 appended hashtags are a legacy habit to drop.**

**Cadence.** Sharpest conflict in the whole dataset: van der Blom/Melanie Goodman says **2–4 posts/week is the sweet spot** (+~1,234 impressions/post), with **daily posting causing a 26% drop in reach per post** and 45% negative compounding from content fatigue. Dataslayer says daily is fine for personal profiles if quality holds. Dharmesh at 2/week with 1.1M followers supports the lower figure. **Encode 3–4/week as default.**

**Timing.** Peak Wednesday 9am; strong Tue–Thu 8–10am audience-local; avoid weekends and Monday morning. Tech/startup audiences skew 7–9am or 6–8pm.

**The golden hour.** 60–90 min window, strongest in first 30 min. Distribution runs in 4 stages: test audience (0–30 min) → extended network (30–90 min) → broad (2–6 hr) → viral (6+ hr). A comment at 20 min may drive 200–500 views vs 10–20 for the same comment at 4 hours (20–50x). **Comment quality is now scored** — "Great post!" is worthless and may be classified as bot behavior. Engagement pods were explicitly targeted in March 2026.

**Reach baseline reset.** Van der Blom's 2025 report: views down ~50% YoY, engagement down 25%, follower growth down 59%. Average reach has "permanently reset to a lower baseline." Only 31% of feed is 1st-degree; 25% 2nd/3rd; 10% suggested. **Set expectations accordingly — a flat post count with falling reach is the platform, not the writer.**

**Suppressed in 2026:** external links; link-in-comment; engagement bait ("Comment 👇", "Agree?"); **polls, collapsed to ~0.07% engagement post-March 2026**; hashtag stacking; generic AI content; pods; "it's not X, it's Y."

### D4. Creator frameworks worth encoding

**Justin Welsh** (500–800K followers, 200K newsletter, $10M+ revenue, ~90% margin). Three-part post: **Trailer** (line 1 breaks the scroll, line 2 builds tension, line 3 promises payoff) → **Meat** (short paras, numbered lists, high scannability) → **CTA/CTC** (a question inviting comments). Pillar mix: mindset 30–35%, tactical how-I-did-it 25–30%, contrarian 20–25%, personal story w/ lesson 15–20%, **direct promotion <5%**. Spends **45 min/day** on comments, ~75% response rate. Hooks: contradiction openers; direct mirrors ("You like. You save. You lurk"); provocative claim + data; numbered-list openers. Core principle: "**Specificity is what makes content feel real rather than generic.**"

**Lara Acosta — SLAY.** **S**top (open on result/contradiction/number, never context) → **L**ayer (one useful idea per paragraph) → **A**nchor (ground in a specific real moment with numbers) → **Y**ou (**one** CTA, never two). Line map: L1 = result + timeframe; L3–6 = one action per line; L7 = anchor w/ real numbers; L8 = single CTA. **Sentences capped at 8–10 words.** Opening formula: "*Specific result in specific timeframe. Here is what worked.*" Sales CTAs go in a pinned first comment, not the body.

**Jasmin Alić.** "**The hook is 90% of a good post.**" Adds the **re-hook** — line 2 must connect to line 1 and "slam the door behind" the reader. Write to one person ("Dear Son" framing). **3–7 pinned comments per post, added after a ~10-minute delay**, containing questions, bonuses, photos, CTAs. Close with a **P.S.** that breaks flow. "Give everything away."

**Matt Barker** (founder-brand ghostwriter). **~131 words average** — notably *shorter* than the 1,300–1,900 char optimum, i.e. optimized for re-reads. 1–2 sentence paragraphs, sentences rarely >15 words, bold first lines, one-line bullets only (no prose disguised as bullets), **P.S. questions instead of aggressive CTAs**. Hook build: base hook → add timeline → add number → add dream outcome → add curiosity/power word. Templates: "How to [outcome] without [obstacle]" / "The #1 best way to [outcome]" / "How to [action] in under [timeframe]" / "Everyone thinks [belief]—here's what actually works." Format mix 58% image, 32% text-only. Carousels 8–24 slides, consistent template, type filling 60–80% of slide. **His own 12 posts/week is explicitly not recommended for founders — he says start at 3–4.**

**Ship 30 for 30 (Nicolas Cole & Dickie Bush) — atomic essay.** **250 words**, published as a visual screenshot. Headline must answer five questions: who is the audience, what's the topic, how does it make them feel, what outcome do you promise, how much info will they get. Then: outline points that pay off each headline promise → expand with story/research/context → format for visual appeal → publish and track. Audience buckets: general / niche / industry — "the size of the question dictates the size of the audience."

**Content pillars (the "3 lanes" synthesis).** The consistent cross-source structure is three lanes: (1) **tactical/how** — what I did and how; (2) **contrarian/analytical** — why the consensus is wrong; (3) **personal story → lesson**. Promotion is a *fourth, minority* lane held under 5–10%. Note: the "4 A's" (Actionable / Analytical / Aspirational / Anthropological) is a real and useful framework but I could **not** verify it originates with Sahil Bloom — it's commonly attributed to him but the sourcing is circular. Encode it as an unattributed framework.

## E. The 2026 X playbook

### E1. Algorithm weights — open-sourced, but read carefully
X open-sourced the ranker again; a **January 2026 Grok transformer layer was added and its scoring logic remains unpublished**, so the open weights are now only a partial picture.

**Heavy Ranker multipliers (relative to Like = 1x):**

| Signal | Weight |
|---|---|
| Like | 1x |
| Bookmark | 10x |
| Link click | 11x |
| Profile click | 12x |
| Reply | 13.5x |
| Retweet | 20x |
| **Reply → author replies back** | **150x** |

That last row is the single most actionable fact on this platform: a reply the author answers is a **7.5x multiplier on top of the already-high reply weight**, and the strongest signal in the ranker. **Answering replies is not etiquette; it is the highest-leverage ranking action available.**

**⚠️ Direct conflict:** a second source reports **replies at 27x a like and retweets at only 1x** — inverting retweet's position entirely. Both cite "the open-source algorithm." Do not encode retweet weight as fact. **What both agree on: replies ≫ likes, and dwell time outweighs all click signals.**

**Pre-ranking filters that kill posts before weights apply:**
1. **Author diversity** — ~3 posts per feed window max from one account.
2. **Negative feedback recency** — blocks/mutes/"show less" in the last 30 days suppress the *entire account*.
3. **Sole-link posts** — outbound URL without original context gets throttled.
4. **Out-of-network caps** — Premium raises the cap.

### E2. Timing and decay
- **First 30 minutes determine ~70% of eventual reach.**
- ~80% of lifetime impressions in the first 2 hours; >80% decay by hour 6; ~zero by 24h.
- Peak windows 8–10am, 12–2pm, 5–7pm ET. Posting at peak = 2–3x reach difference.
- Engage your own early replies within the first 15 minutes.
- Account-level performance affects future reach for **30+ days**.

### E3. Length and format
- 280 chars free; **25,000 for Premium**.
- Sub-100-char posts get higher engagement *rates*; longer posts get deeper replies. **⚠️ Conflict:** one source says single long posts beat threads for most uses (threads add algorithmic friction and context loss); another says threads get 3–5x reach. Reconciliation that fits both: **threads only for genuinely sequential narratives; a single long-form post otherwise.**
- Thread completion by length: **5–7 tweets = 70%**; 8–10 = 50%; 11–15 = 30%; 16+ = 15%. Individual tweets 1–2 sentences / 100–150 chars. Number them (1/7) to set expectations.
- Native video +40–60% reach vs text; images +15–25%; external links −30–40%; polls high-variance.
- **Link workaround still works on X** (unlike LinkedIn): URL in the first reply, not the main post.

### E4. Premium reach — the best data available
Buffer analyzed **18.8M posts across 71,000 accounts** (Aug 2024–Aug 2025):
- **Premium accounts get ~10x more reach per post.**
- Regular: <100 impressions/post. Premium Basic: minimal lift. Premium: ~600. **Premium+: 1,550+.**
- Engagement *rates* are nearly flat across tiers (0.49–0.55%) — **Premium buys distribution, not engagement quality.**
- **Half of non-Premium accounts received zero interactions by March 2025**; link posts from non-Premium accounts showed 0% engagement.
- Baseline decline platform-wide: median reach ~1,000 → <750 over the year.

**Conclusion: on X in 2026, Premium is effectively a prerequisite, not an optimization.** (A separate source's "10–25% reply lift" figure is far more conservative than Buffer's 10x; Buffer has the bigger sample and clearer method.)

### E5. Reply-guy strategy — growing from zero
The consensus method, with numbers:
- **70/30 rule:** 70% of time on strategic replies, 30% original posts. Tracked accounts: 500–2,000 followers/month.
- **Volume:** 15–20 quality replies/day is optimal (→100–200 profile visits, 20–40 followers/week). Aggressive: 30–50. **Stay under 50/day** to avoid spam detection; hard limit ~500.
- **Speed is everything:** reply within **5 minutes** of the post going up. 5–15 min good; 15–30 min reduced; **skip anything 30+ min old.** The first 5–10 replies get the visibility.
- **Target sizing — sources conflict.** One says target accounts **5–20x your follower count** and avoid 500K+ mega-accounts (replies get buried). Another says target **50K+ minimum**, 50–100 of them, notifications on. *The 5–20x rule is more defensible for a genuinely-zero account.*
- **Reply craft:** 1–3 sentences, standalone-comprehensible, adds new information, never just agreement. **Data-containing replies get 3.2x more engagement; experience-based replies convert 2.8x better to profile visits.** No emoji-only, no links, no generic praise, no long paragraphs.
- **Deboost triggers:** 20+ replies in one hour; near-identical replies; >3–4 replies to one account per day; links in replies. **Recovery:** stop 24h, resume at 5–10/day, full visibility back in 48–72h.
- **Timeline:** traction 2–4 weeks; momentum 2–3 months; at 1,000–5,000 followers, shift from replying-outward to engaging your own audience.
- Profile click rate >5% correlates with 30%+ follower conversion on a viral post — **so the bio and pinned post are part of the reply strategy.**

### E6. X and AI content
Weaker and more contradictory than LinkedIn's.
- **Feb 22, 2026:** head of product **Nikita Bier** announced user-applied **AI-content tags** plus enhanced bot detection/removal.
- Bier: "People come to X to get a pulse on humanity… there is nothing more unsettling than expecting you're reading the words of a human, only to find it was a machine." He had earlier called AI bot profiles "the scourge of X."
- X says product, policies and approach "will need to evolve meaningfully."
- **The contradiction to encode:** X simultaneously promotes Grok as an in-app post-composition tool. There is **no evidence of an X ranking penalty on AI-assisted writing** comparable to LinkedIn's. The enforcement target on X is *bot accounts and inauthentic automation*, not AI-assisted human posting. Automation rate limits are the real constraint.

## F. Encodable rules for the ghostwriting skill

**Universal**
1. Human supplies the claim — a specific number, named failure, client outcome, or decision + reasoning. AI supplies research, structure, phrasing, variants. Never let the model invent the central assertion.
2. Ban the "it's not X, it's Y" construction outright — LinkedIn names it as a classifier tell.
3. Ban: "In today's fast-paced world," "Let that sink in," "Here's the thing," "Agree?", "Thoughts?", "Comment 👇", generic-praise comments.
4. Every draft must pass: *could only this person have written this?* If no, reject.
5. First line must be self-contained within 140 characters and carry a specific claim, number, or tension.
6. Never draft political/geopolitical content in a founder voice without explicit sign-off.

**LinkedIn**
7. Target 1,300–1,900 chars. Line-break every 1–2 sentences. Sentences under 15 words.
8. Zero hashtags by default; max 3 if the client insists.
9. No links in body. No links in first comment (dead as of 2026). Route to native article/newsletter, or publish clean and edit the link in at 2–3 hours.
10. 3–4 posts/week, Tue–Thu 8–10am audience-local. Not daily.
11. Prefer document/carousel (5–10 slides) > multi-image > video (30–90s, captioned) > text. But plain text at Dharmesh scale still works — format is not a substitute for a claim.
12. One CTA maximum, preferably a P.S. question. No engagement bait. No polls.
13. Reserve the 45–60 min after publishing for substantive replies; author replies are ranking fuel.
14. Hold topic consistency tight — the retrieval model matches embeddings, so drift costs reach directly.

**X**
15. Premium/Premium+ is a prerequisite (~10x reach).
16. Single long-form post by default; thread only for sequential narrative, capped at 5–7 tweets.
17. Links in the first reply, never the main post.
18. Reply to every reply — the 150x author-response multiplier is the strongest signal in the ranker.
19. Max ~3 posts per feed window; post at peak hours; the first 30 min decide 70% of reach.
20. From zero: 70/30 replies-to-posts, 15–20 replies/day, within 5 min, to accounts 5–20x your size, 1–3 sentences carrying data or first-hand experience.
