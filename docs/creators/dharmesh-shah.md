He opens on a habit he has kept for decades, then shows the real request he typed into his product and what it did. He answers the "why not just ChatGPT" question himself and gives the price last. It's almost 500 words and got about twice the engagement of any other post.

## The most comments came from a guessing game

<img src="{{ '/creators/img/dharmesh-shah/post-ladder.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Dharmesh Shah post: considering switching from Google Slides back to PowerPoint. 1,020 reactions, 277 comments." width="100%" style="max-width:520px;border-radius:8px;display:block;margin:8px 0">

He asks why he would switch, rules out the likely reasons one by one, and gives the real one in a line: AI tools work better with that file format. It got 277 comments, the most in the set, and many of them were readers guessing along.

Same-day news gets reactions and few comments. A new name for something (AI Foam, Total Cost of Outcomes, idea taste) or a confession under a quote gets nearly three times as many comments per reaction. The groups are small, two to nine posts each, so read this as a direction.

## What a founder in AI memory could try this week

1. Paste one real request you gave your own memory system and show what came back.
2. When a lab ships something about memory, post within hours and keep it short. Write the longer take the next day.
3. Give a name to a failure everyone has seen, like an agent forgetting a fact, and define it in one sentence.
4. Tell a user's story in your own words instead of resharing their praise.
5. Put the price in. In his posts the hard sell in his own voice did fine.
