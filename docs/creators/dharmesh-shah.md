# Dharmesh Shah, teardown of 45 LinkedIn posts

Dharmesh Shah co-founded HubSpot and is its CTO. He is also building YouSpot, an AI-native personal CRM for solo operators, in public. The core idea is a "second brain" that you own. That puts him squarely in AI memory and personal context. He also has 1,202,381 LinkedIn followers, so he is a useful case of a large account selling a new product to the same feed it built its reputation on.

What I read: his 45 most recent original posts on LinkedIn, counting quote-shares and skipping plain reposts. I read them on 7 November 2026, logged in, from his activity page. The newest was 21 hours old and LinkedIn labels the oldest "1mo". "Engagement" below means reactions plus comments, as shown on the page that day. One post about company news is counted in the numbers but not discussed.

## What the numbers say

- Median: 315 reactions, 74 comments, 389 engagement per post.
- Spread: the bottom quarter is under 175 and the top quarter is above 691.
- Top three: a story about using his product to plan a dinner guest list (3,328 reactions, 228 comments), a launch post for a Gmail connector (1,568 / 205), and a post about switching from Google Slides to PowerPoint (1,020 / 277).
- Quote-shares of other people praising YouSpot did worst as a group: a median of 129 engagement across seven posts, against 470 for his native posts.
- By my count, 18 native posts had an image and 20 did not. The ones with an image had a median of 603, the ones without 374. The ones I looked at were product screenshots or a simple branded graphic, not photos.
- 21 of the 45 posts mention YouSpot. Product posts land both in his top quarter and in his bottom quarter, so the product is not what decides the result. The shape is.

## Five shapes that carry him

**1. The long-running habit, then the tool.** His best post by far opens on a personal ritual he has kept for decades, not on the product. He names the annoying part of it, pastes the exact request he gave the product, answers the "couldn't I just use ChatGPT" objection himself, and only then gives the price. It runs nearly 500 words and still beat everything else by about two times. Showing the real prompt is the specificity move: anyone can see the task, and nobody else had that task.

**2. "BREAKING NEWS:" on the day a frontier lab ships.** Three posts at or above his top-quarter line are same-day reactions to a model or product launch. Each has a label in capitals, one quoted line from the launch, three short bullets or lines, an ownership disclosure, and a promise to say more later. The one news post that grew into a four-point essay got 131 engagement, the lowest of the news group. Short and first works. Long and thoughtful on news does not.

**3. The ladder of wrong reasons.** "Considering switching from X to Y. Why?" Then two or three guesses, each knocked down in brackets, then the real reason in one line (AI tools work better with one file format). After that comes a lesson for software companies and a short plug in a p.s. This one drew 277 comments, the most of any post in the set. The reader plays the guessing game in the comments.

**4. Coin a phrase, define it in one line.** AI Foam. Total Cost of Outcomes. An acronym that replaces WYSIWYG. Idea taste versus product taste. These sit in the middle by reactions (150 to 390). They pull a high share of comments, though, 30 to 55 per 100 reactions, against about 23 for a post at his median (74 comments on 315 reactions). A named idea gives people something to argue with.

**5. "Woo hoo!" plus three questions you could ask it.** His launch posts that worked do not list features. They list three example questions in quotes that a user could now ask the product. The Gmail-connector post did this and reached 1,773 engagement. The Granola integration post walked through what happens right after you connect, step by step, and also landed in the top quarter (719).

## What does not work for him

Quote-shares of testimonials, from 19 to 173 engagement, are at the bottom. So are event invitations (175), and book quotes with only a line or two added (165 and 334, both under his median). A book quote works when he turns it on himself with a confession about his own weakness. The writing post built that way got 145 comments. The pattern is clear: when the post is someone else's words with his approval attached, the feed treats it as an ad.

## Cadence and habits

- He posts more than once on some days. The original posts are mixed with plain reposts of customers and partners.
- Length runs from 19 to 495 words. His best post is his longest, and two posts under 50 words beat his median (900 and 555), so length is not the lever.
- Habits: a "p.s." in 6 of 45 posts, usually for the plug, and an ownership disclosure when he talks about a company he has invested in. He uses double hyphens, asterisks for emphasis, and an occasional word in capitals. Hashtags are almost absent. Links in the body are nearly always promo links. Few posts end on a direct question to the reader. Most end on a flat line or a p.s.
- He repeats the product's category in brackets nearly every time he names it, so a stranger landing on any single post knows what it is.

## What a founder in AI memory could borrow this week

1. **Show the prompt, not the feature.** Take one real request you gave your own memory system this week, paste it, and show what came back. In this set the real prompt beat the feature list every time.
2. **Be first on launch day, and keep it short.** When a lab ships something that touches memory or context, post within hours: what shipped, three lines on what it means for people building agents, and what you own or have tested. Save the essay for the next day.
3. **Name the problem you keep explaining.** If you have a phrase for a failure everyone has seen, like an agent forgetting a fact or memory locked inside one app, define it in one sentence and stop there. The comments will do the rest.
4. **Skip the testimonial reshare.** If a user says something good, ask what they did with the product and tell that story yourself, with your own screenshot.
5. **Put the category in brackets every time.** Most of your readers will see one post, not the series.
