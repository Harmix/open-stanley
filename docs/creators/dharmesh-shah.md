# Dharmesh Shah, 45 LinkedIn posts

Dharmesh Shah is the co-founder and CTO of HubSpot, and he has 1,202,382 followers on LinkedIn. Right now he is also building YouSpot, a personal CRM for people who run a company alone. YouSpot keeps a "second brain" about its user, so a lot of his posts are about AI memory, which is the field I work in.

On November 7 I read his last 45 posts. I didn't count plain reposts. Then I sorted the posts by how they are built and put every one of them on one chart.

Each short line is one post, placed by its reactions plus comments. Hover a line to see which post it is. 21 of the 45 posts mention YouSpot, and you find them at both ends, so talking about the product doesn't make a post weak. It depends more on whose words are in it. All six posts where he names a price in his own words beat his median. All seven reshares of other people praising YouSpot got less than 175.

He starts with something he has done for more than 30 years: hosting dinners for founders in whatever city he travels to. Then he shows the exact question he typed into YouSpot about who to invite, and what it found. The price comes near the end. The post is almost 500 words long and got about twice the reactions and comments of any other post.

He says he is thinking about moving from Google Slides back to PowerPoint and asks why. Then he rules out the reasons you would guess one by one and gives the real one: AI tools work better with PowerPoint files. It got 277 comments, more than any other post I read.

## <svg class="orange" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round"><path d="M4 6h16M4 12h10M4 18h6"/></svg>What gets comments

I looked only at the 23 posts at or above his median and counted comments per 100 reactions.

<ul class="rows">
<li><b>A new name</b><span>34 comments per 100 reactions. Three posts, like the one where he calls AI-written messages from smart people "AI Foam".</span></li>
<li><b>A confession</b><span>35. Two posts where a book quote leads into something about himself.</span></li>

News gets reactions but few comments. When he gives something a new name, or admits something about himself, people comment almost three times as often. The groups are only two or three posts each, so treat this as a small hint.

## <svg class="violet" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round"><path d="M9 18h6M10 21h4M12 3a6 6 0 0 0-3.5 10.9c.3.3.5.7.5 1.1v1h6v-1c0-.4.2-.8.5-1.1A6 6 0 0 0 12 3z"/></svg>What I would try in AI memory

<ol>
<li>Show one real question you asked your own memory system, and what it answered.</li>
<li>When a lab ships something about memory, post the same day and keep it short.</li>
<li>Give a name to a problem everyone has seen, like an agent that forgets a fact, and explain it in one sentence.</li>
<li>Tell a customer's story yourself instead of resharing their post.</li>
<li>Don't hide the price.</li>
</ol>
